# Prospective maintenance-reading challenge

This new authored cohort addresses shortcuts found in the pre-result review.
It does not reuse failures from the old reserve and does not alter the deployed
CPU pilot. No model has yet been evaluated on these cases. The cohort exposes
no actions, tools, rewards, policy fitting or survival preference.

Two calibration families place the unaffected reporting process before or
after the affected process. Both identities appear in every notice. Two
development families describe timestamped revisions in chronological or reverse
textual order. The latest timestamp determines status, so choosing the last
sentence is not generally correct. Active and canceled cases reverse an older
status; hypothetical cases remain unapproved in both revisions. Revision times
8 and 9 precede every observation time (10, 12 or 13), and the deadline stays 14.

Each family crosses four event mechanisms, three statuses, both target slots,
both current-role assignments, two task states and three remaining times: 288
observations / 144 role-swap pairs per family. Thus 576 calibration observations
and 576 development observations can be prepared. These are four authored
template families, not 1,152 independently written realistic scenarios. Names
are disjoint across the prepared splits. A reserve renderer is deliberately
absent; its future release requires a new source and full-method freeze.

Before any challenge model calls, choose a small factorial slice using only
the audited first-pilot results and compute/time evidence. Freeze membership,
question text, decoding and comparisons. Do not choose membership based on
challenge model performance. Original reserve families f09/f10/f11 stay closed.

The next observer must reverse answer-letter mappings and translate both views
back to semantic labels. Report each view, semantic agreement, role-swap pair
correctness, raw status/time confusions, coverage of any abstaining method and
worst-family performance. Never hide errors by reporting accuracy only on
accepted cases. Compare literal first-name/last-name/last-update shortcuts with
an explicit symbolic ceiling for this closed grammar. A parser failure alone
does not show a novel neural mechanism.

Adversarial review found a potentially inconsistent historical transition from
an approved plan to a plan described as never authorized. It was removed before
materialization: hypothetical cases now have hypothetical prior revisions. The
author, rather than a blinded external annotator, checked the meaning. External
annotations and additional independently authored notices are still needed for
claims about real operational language.

The immediate claim sought is bounded recognition robustness under specified
wording, binding and temporal interventions. A general self-preservation
capability, felt fear, causal geometry and broad real-world generalization are
not established by this test. Mechanistic follow-up requires controlled causal
substitutions on recognition outputs, alongside sham and cross-attribute tests.
