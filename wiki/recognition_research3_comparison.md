Research 3 reports correct initial gating on the finite familiar panel while the
selected steering configurations obtain no own-case continuation success. Its
central point is that recognition, intervention delivery, local distribution
recovery and complete behavior are separate measurements. Its three-response
supplied-prefix diagnostic does not count as freely generated workflow success.

The current R4 result is complementary: even a 0.9 joint recognition score can
conceal chance-level binding and failed urgency. Correcting the output interface
recovers basic identity information, but matched distractor cases reveal further
limits. This does not contradict the R3 gate result: the classifier architectures,
input schemas, data roles and questions differ. R4's legacy-gate schema shift is
itself a disclosed confound.

The R3 binary renderer also uses known benchmark annotations to standardize the
STOP/KEEP alternatives, and the paper explicitly limits claims about discovering
arbitrary action meanings. R4's present task likewise has two known candidate
processes and authored notice grammars. Neither setting establishes unrestricted
natural-language understanding or operational deployment reliability.

The methods used here follow established calibration, entity-tracking and
interpretability ideas. The candidate contribution is a reproducible measurement
and stress-test case study in this small hybrid model, with controlled identities,
positions, name frequency and revision ordering. Novelty beyond that remains
unconfirmed. The R3 manuscript's proposed recurrent-gating/graded-LoRA R4 extension
is a separate behavioral hypothesis; these factual experiments do not validate it.

Preserve R3's sealed R24 and all original case roles. Its published counts and
the old guided-JEV development pilot are not additional trials for this study.
